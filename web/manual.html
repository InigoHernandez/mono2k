Start here

MONO2K turns the M-VAVE FM-1 into a small Monomachine: six mono machines, a 64-step sequencer that locks any knob to any step, and a lofi bus that breaks the sound on purpose.

On the FM-1

Hold GLO, HOME, EDIT, SEQ, FX or LFO a moment: the screen lists what each does with the other buttons. GLO > HELP is this manual's short version, on the device.

Install

From The Firmware Hub or the installer, over USB, in Chrome or Edge. Or try it in the browser first.

  1. Back up what your FM-1 holds: installing other firmware is at your own risk.
  2. Open the installer (or The Firmware Hub) in Chrome or Edge and connect the FM-1 by USB.
  3. Press Install. The FM-1 restarts into MONO2K; coming from an older MONO2K, your patterns stay.

Every FM-1

It runs on the first units' flash (0x856014) and on the newer chip of units sold since autumn 2026 (0xB36014), since 1.9.2. Holding OCT− while the FM-1 powers on always reaches the installer; M-VAVE's own updater, or the installer's return to the official firmware, takes it back to how it shipped.

What it keeps

The FM-1 keeps one project of 64 patterns (bank A the demos, B–D INIT, room for some 20 to 30 patterns of your own beside the demos; it says when it is full) and the 128 sounds, in its flash. The emulator runs the same firmware in the browser and keeps four projects of 64. When the save format grows, older saves are converted as they load.

Note

MONO2K is not affiliated with Elektron or M-VAVE. Monomachine is Elektron's; FM-1 is M-VAVE's. MONO2K's machines are written from scratch and measured against a Monomachine's output; none of Elektron's code or samples are in it.

The browser emulator

The FM-1 drawn in one bit, its screen, and the firmware running underneath.

Open the emulator and press Start to turn the sound on. Click the panel first so it takes the keyboard; Esc leaves it. Drag or scroll a knob to turn it. The emulator runs at 44.1 kHz, as the FM-1 does, and saves your work in the browser as you go.

MIDI and saving

A MIDI keyboard or controller plugged into the computer plays the emulator as it would the FM-1 over USB (see MIDI). Under the panel, the project's tools export a project as a .m2kp file and import one into any of the four project slots.

Find your way

Four ideas run the whole instrument. Learn these and every page reads the same.

  1. A button opens its page; pressed again, its next page. EDIT the machine, ENV the amp, SEL the filter, FX the effects, LFO, ARP, GLO, HOME. The footer says where you are: track ▸ machine ▸ page.
  2. KNOB 1–4 turn the page. SELECT picks the track, ALGORITHM its machine, PRESETS a sound (on HOME and GLO, a pattern), on every page.
  3. Hold a button for its layer. GLO, HOME, SEQ, EDIT, FX and LFO turn the keys and knobs into something else while held, and the screen lists what. Double-tap one to keep it.
  4. REC copies, PLAY pastes, SEQ clears. The button you hold says what: GLO the pattern, EDIT the sound, a held step that step. GLO + OCT− undoes.

Play the demos

Bank A holds four finished parts. Hear what MONO2K does, then take one apart.

  1. Press PLAY: A01 plays.
  2. On HOME, turn PRESETS: the next pattern waits for the end of the bar, so the parts chain as you play.
  3. Hold GLO and tap white 1–6: each track mutes, and comes back, at the next bar. Hold white 9–14 to solo.
  4. Turn SELECT through the tracks and press EDIT: each one's machine, its card and its knobs.
  5. Hold FX and press a black key: the whole mix repeats, reverses or stops while you hold it.

Bank A

Four parts, each a 4-bar pattern that plays as a 16-bar form: what comes in after the first pass, what alternates, what arrives on the third and fourth. Every sound in them was picked by ear, then fixed by measurement until it sat in the tempo and the harmony. A05–A16 are blank, ready for yours.

The demos back

On the FM-1, GLO > SET, turn DEMO, OCT+ for YES: A01–A04 as they shipped; A05–A16 and banks B–D are kept.

Build a beat

A new pattern, a kick, a snare and hats, in a minute.

  1. Hold HOME, press black 2 (G#3) for bank B, and tap a white key: an INIT pattern opens, six sounds ready: a sine bass, a kick, a snare, a hat, FM keys and a pad.
  2. Press SEQ: GRID in the header. The lower keys are now the 16 steps.
  3. Pick the kick's track with SELECT (or the upper keys T1–T6) and tap steps 1, 5, 9 and 13.
  4. Press PLAY. Pick the snare's track: 5 and 13. The hat's: every other step.
  5. A quick tap on a trig takes it away. SAVE keeps the pattern.

Other sounds

  • PRESETS on any page but HOME and GLO: each sound of the library goes on the track as you land on it. Stop there and it stays (3 s, or any button); OCT− puts your own back.
  • ALGORITHM: another machine, at its INIT, a sound made for what it is for.
  • More in Shape a sound.

GRID

SEQ turns the keys into steps; SEQ again gives the notes back. The sixteen lower keys are the steps of the page in view. A key on an empty step puts a trig there with the last note you played; a quick tap on a trig takes it away, with its locks. The upper keys: T1–T6 pick the track, P1–P4 the page (held: the length), F#5 mutes the track.

Or play it in: PADS

  1. GLO + HOME (or GLO + ARP): PADS in the header. White keys 1–6 (and 9–14 for the other hand) hit tracks 1–6, each at its root, the note its trigs sit on most, so a drum stays in the key it is tuned to.
  2. Press PLAY, tap REC and play: each hit is written to the nearest step of its track.
  3. GLO + HOME again goes on to GRID, then KEYS; GLO + ARP or SEQ leaves PADS at once.

Tempo and swing

  • HOME: KNOB 3 the tempo, KNOB 4 the swing. GLO + SELECT turns the tempo from any page.
  • GLO + white 8: tap the tempo, two taps or more, on the beat.

A slip

  • GLO + OCT− undoes the last edit; GLO + OCT+ does it again.
  • Hold REC and tap SEQ: the track in view's steps are gone, its sound stays. SEQ again brings them back.
  • Hold GLO and press SEQ: the whole pattern's steps go, the sounds stay. Keep SEQ down a second for INIT.

Add a bass line

Notes on a track: played in, written step by step, or set on each step.

  1. Pick the bass's track with SELECT. If GRID is in the header, press SEQ: the keys play the track.
  2. OCT− and OCT+ set its octave; each track keeps its own.
  3. Stopped, tap REC: STEP ENTRY. Each note you play goes to the next step; EDIT leaves a rest. REC again to leave.
  4. Or press PLAY, tap REC and play along: notes land on the nearest step.
  5. In GRID, hold a step and turn SELECT: its note, heard as you turn.

Recording

  • Live. With the keys playing notes (not GRID), press PLAY and tap REC. Notes land on the nearest step; a hard key records an accent; a knob turned just after a trig locks its value to that step. Tap REC again to stop. A take you don't want: hold REC and tap SEQ.
  • Ready. Stopped with REC on, tap PLAY: READY, PLAY blinks, nothing moves. The first note you play (a key, a pad, MIDI) starts the pattern from its first step and is recorded there. PLAY again starts it at once. Following a MIDI clock, PLAY plays as ever.
  • Step entry. Stopped, tap REC: each note goes to the next step and the cursor moves on. EDIT leaves a rest; PRESETS moves the cursor back and on.

Notes and octaves

Each track plays at home. A machine chosen or INITed puts the track's new trigs, its pad and its keys on the note its INIT was made at: DRM-KICK at C2, GND-SIN, FM+DYN and the FX at C3, the rest at C4. A pattern opened puts each track on its root, the note its trigs sit on most. The keyboard's octave is each track's own, set from its home note: a kick at C2 plays as a kick, not as a high zap. OCT− and OCT+ change the track in view's, and their lights show it.

Accent, slide and length

  • Hold a step and turn ALGORITHM: no accent, accent, slide, both. An accent plays harder (how much: GLO > TIME, ACNT); a slide glides into the next note without trigging again.
  • A long note: hold a step a moment, then press a later empty step. The note lasts to it, up to 8 steps: LEN on the info line, a dash through the steps. A nearer key shortens it; the same last key again gives the sound's own length back. The next trig on the track still cuts it.

The arpeggiator

On ARP: MODE (OFF, UP, DOWN, U/D, RND, ORDR, the order you played them), SPD (1/32 to 1/4), RNG (one to four octaves) and GATE. Hold keys and it plays them in turn.

  • HOLD: tap ARP twice (or press a sustain pedal, CC 64): the notes keep playing after you let go; a chord played after your hands leave the keys replaces them. ARP twice again stops it. Its LED pulses while it holds.
  • An arp a track: pick another track and a held one plays on; build a second there. On HOME a small staircase marks each lane whose arp holds. PLAY starts every arp with the pattern; STOP and a new pattern leave the holds playing.
  • Recorded: with REC on while playing, every playing arp writes its notes into its track's steps, one a step.

Chords on one track

SWAV-ENS plays three more voices at their intervals over each note: a chord, a pad or a stab from one key, on one track. Its INIT is a minor-7 pad.

Shape a sound

Each track is one machine and its chain. Three pages make most of the sound: SYN, AMP and FILT.

  1. Pick the track with SELECT. Turn ALGORITHM for another machine: its card comes up, its INIT ready to play.
  2. EDIT: SYN, the machine's own eight knobs, A–D, then E–H (EDIT again).
  3. ENV: AMP, the note's shape. SEL: FILT, the filters.
  4. Gone too far? Hold OCT− and turn the knob: it goes back to its INIT.
  5. Hold EDIT and press SAVE: the sound goes into the library.

Machines

ALGORITHM changes the track's machine, on any page. A track still at its machine's INIT takes the new machine's whole INIT; a sound you have shaped keeps its chain, its LFOs and its steps, and only the eight knobs start from the new machine's. The order: the ten that play notes first (GND-SIN, SWAV-SAW, SWAV-PUL, SWAV-ENS, FM+STAT, FM+PAR, FM+DYN, VOX-6, DIGI-WAV, SID-6581), then the drums (DRM-KICK, DRM-SNAR, DRM-HATS, DRM-CYMB), GND-PERC and GND-NOIS, and the three FX last. Each machine's knobs: Machines.

INIT: each machine's own sound

Every machine starts as a sound for what it is for: GND-SIN a round sine bass, SWAV-SAW a wide supersaw pad, SWAV-PUL a hollow reed lead, SWAV-ENS a minor-7 pad on one key, FM+STAT an e-piano, FM+PAR a gong-like bell, FM+DYN an FM bass, VOX-6 an "aah", DIGI-WAV a pluck that scans as it falls, SID-6581 a sync lead, the DRM- drums a kick, a snare, a closed hat and a crash, GND-PERC a metal hit, GND-NOIS a wind you play. Each is levelled to sit with the others. INIT is what ALGORITHM gives a track at its INIT, what EDIT + SEQ and an INIT slot of the library start a track with, and where OCT− and a knob take that knob back.

Amp: an ADSR

AMP 1 is ATCK DEC SUS REL, AMP 2 DIST VOL PAN PORT, AMP 3 HOLD: press ENV again for each. The times are the ones you hear.

  • ATCK rises to full, as the square of its time, evenly to the ear: 20 ms at 16, 67 ms at 32, 0.4 s at 64, 2 s at 96, 10 s at 127; 0 is a percussive 1 ms.
  • DEC falls to SUS's level: 0.14 s at 32, 0.8 s at 64, 4 s at 96, 20 s at the top; at 127 it stays at full while the note lasts.
  • SUS is the level held while the note lasts, shown in dB. At 0 there is none: the note falls to silence over DEC however long it is, the way a drum does and every sound made before 1.10 does.
  • REL takes it down once the note ends, a key let go or the step's length run out, from wherever it is.
  • A note's length: a key, as long as you hold it; a trig, the track's GATE (ARP's fourth knob; just under a step at INIT), or as long as you make it: hold the step, press a later one (Accent, slide and length).
  • HOLD (AMP 3) keeps full level before the decay. It counts in beats at the pattern's tempo (32 a beat, 8 a sixteenth); 127 holds while the note lasts.
  • DIST drives the machine into a clip of its own, VOL sets the track's level into the chain, PAN places it, PORT glides between notes.

A note played over one still sounding rises from where that one is.

Filter

A high-pass and a low-pass as one band: BASE is the bottom of the band, WDTH its width (at the top, BASE alone is a high-pass; at the bottom, WDTH alone is a low-pass), HPQ and LPQ their resonance. The filter follows the note.

  • FILT 2 is its envelope, an ADSR too: ATCK and DEC, SUS the share held while the note lasts, REL its fall once the note ends. At SUS 0 it rises and falls to zero whatever the note's length, as before 1.10.
  • FILT 3: B-OF and W-OF, how far the envelope moves the base and the width, either way.

Level, pitch, mute

On HOME, KNOB 1 and 2 are the track's level and pitch. In GRID the MUTE key mutes the track in view; GLO held with white 1–6 mutes a track from anywhere.

Keep it, find it again: the sound library

128 sounds, S001 to S128, kept apart from the patterns: a sound saved there can go onto any track of any pattern. A sound is a machine and every setting, a category (KICK, SNARE, HATS, PERC, BASS, LEAD, PAD or FX) and the note it was made at, so a kick saved at C2 plays at C2. The library starts with 96 sounds of its own, twelve in each category, then 32 INIT slots.

  • Find: turn PRESETS on any page but HOME and GLO: each sound is on the track as you land on it; stop and it stays (3 s, or any button). Or hold EDIT a moment for the same list that asks: KNOB 2 the category, OCT+ keeps, anything else puts yours back. OCT− always gives the track its own back; GLO + OCT− undoes.
  • Keep: hold EDIT and press SAVE. It goes to the first INIT slot, in the category it sounds like.
  • File it yourself: GLO + EDIT (SYN > SND), turn CAT to a category, pick an INIT slot with SLOT, turn SAVE. Over a sound it asks first: OCT+ YES, OCT− NO.
  • Start afresh: hold EDIT and press SEQ.

Copy a sound

Hold EDIT and press REC: the track's sound (its machine and every setting) is copied. Hold EDIT and press PLAY on another track, in this pattern or another: pasted.

Add effects

Three places: on each track, on the bus where the tracks meet, and under your hands.

  1. FX: the track's own effects. Press FX again for DELAY, DSTR and MOD.
  2. On DSTR, turn VERB: the track goes to the bus's reverb.
  3. Press GLO until the footer says BUS: DCAY the reverb's length, DAMP how dark it falls, ADPC a 4-bit crunch, DRIV the drive.
  4. Hold FX: PERFORM. Each black key an effect on the whole mix, while held.

On the track: FX

  • FX: EQF and EQG, one band of EQ; SRR reduces the sample rate.
  • DELAY: the track's own (DTIM, DSND, DFB, DBAS, DWID), in 8-bit mu-law.
  • DSTR: BITS crushes, FOLD folds the wave over itself, XOR mangles the samples, VERB sends the track to the bus's reverb.
  • MOD: a chorus, a flanger or a phaser across the stereo.

Both sends, DSND and VERB, are straight in level: half way is 6 dB under the most, so a little is already heard.

On the bus: reverb, crunch, drive

  • DCAY: 0.3 s (a small room) to 24 s, 0 off. Each track sends to it with DSTR's VERB.
  • DAMP: 1/1 keeps the top as long as the bottom (bright), 1/30 lets it die thirty times sooner (dark).
  • ADPC: the 4-bit crunch comes in up to 64, then its rate falls.
  • DRIV: into the soft clip, never past full scale.

The bus's settings belong to the pattern, so each part of a song can be worn differently.

A track as an effect: FX machines

FX-CHORUS, FX-RING and FX-DYNAMIX make no sound of their own: each takes the track before it (after that track's amp) and works on it. Put the source on track 3 and the FX on track 4; the FX hears the source before its VOL, so turn the source's VOL down to hear only the effect. The Monomachine's metal sounds are made this way: a plucky FM+PAR into FX-CHORUS with its feedback all the way up.

Under your hands: PERFORM

Hold FX: the black keys repeat, reverse, stop, freeze, filter or kill the whole mix while held. Its two banks: Play it live.

Add movement

A sound that changes as it plays: a knob locked to one step, an LFO, envelopes that run on.

  1. Hold GLO, press LFO, then turn any knob of the track: an LFO now moves it, with some depth so you hear it at once.
  2. Press LFO: its rate (SPD), depth (DPTH) and wave (WAVE).
  3. In GRID, hold a step and turn a knob: that value plays on that step alone, a lock.
  4. Hold LFO and sweep a knob: it comes back when you let go. OCT+ while held keeps it.

Parameter locks

Hold a step in GRID and turn any of the four knobs, on any page: the value is locked to that step and plays for it alone. The Monomachine's way of making one track sound like many.

  • The knob shows the lock in reverse, and the step's cell marks it.
  • A step can carry locks on as many parameters as you like, up to the pattern's pool: a machine's knobs, the chain, the LFOs.
  • Hold OCT− and turn a knob: that lock away. Taking the trig away takes its locks with it.
  • Recording live, a knob turned just after a trig locks its value to that step.
  • To lock without trigging anything, give the step the trig type NONE (below).

LFOs

Three per track. LFO, LFO again and LFO again give each one's DEST, WAVE, SPD and DPTH; a fourth press their rest, TRCK, TRIG, MULT and PHAS. On LFO's pages SELECT picks LFO 1, 2 or 3.

  • GLO + LFO: the next knob you turn, on any page of any track, is what the LFO moves (the track's first free LFO, or the one in view). The LED blinks until you turn one; 10 s, or LFO, lets it go. A knob an LFO moves shows a marker swinging under its picture.
  • TRCK and DEST: which track and parameter it moves. WAVE its shape. TRIG what a trig does to it: FREE runs on, TRIG starts it over, HOLD samples it until the next trig, ONE runs one cycle, HALF half a cycle. PHAS where it starts.
  • SPD shows the time one cycle takes, in bars and notes: 1 is 30 bars, 12 is 16 bars, 60 a bar, 84 a quarter note, 120 a 1/32 note; 0 stands still. Every twelve steps it doubles; seven slower than a note is that note dotted, seven faster its triplet. MULT multiplies it, x1 to x128.
  • DPTH swings either way from 64, fine near the middle (66 an eighth of a step, 72 two, 80 eight), the whole range at the ends. A little on PTCH is a vibrato (66 is about 12 cents).

The LFOs run on MONO2K's own clock, 1.5 % slower than the bars they are named after: a FREE LFO drifts slowly against the pattern, and TRIG starts it with each note. Whatever an LFO moves glides between the knob's steps, with no zipper.

Trig types

The Monomachine's magic: a step can restart the amp, the filter's envelope and the LFOs independently, and leave the others running. Change the pitch under a filter sweep that keeps going, restart only the LFO for a new wobble, or move the note with no new attack at all. Hold a step in GRID and turn MASTER (in the browser, /), or press black 1–5: ALL, AMP, FILTER, LFO, NONE.

Every type moves the pitch to the step's note and applies the step's locks. The machine's own envelopes (an FM+ operator falling from the trig, GND-SIN's pitch drop) restart with the amp. Without AMP the note's gate runs on as it was: if the amp has already died away, a FILTER or LFO trig changes a sound you cannot hear. Only the LFOs set to restart on a trig restart (TRIG). Ratchets repeat the step with its type; notes played on the keys or over MIDI always trig ALL.

  • A bass line under one long filter sweep: ALL on the first step, AMP on the others.
  • A held pad whose chord changes under one envelope: NONE on the changes.
  • A wobble that starts again on the off-beats: LFO on the steps between the notes.

Make it vary

Steps that play by chance, by loop or on a fill; tracks of their own length; tools that move the beat.

  1. In GRID, hold a step and press black 7: it plays half the time (50%).
  2. Hold a step and press OCT+: a ratchet, two hits in the step.
  3. Hold SEQ and press white 3 or 4: the track's steps move a step earlier or later.
  4. Hold SEQ and turn KNOB 2: the track's own length. 12 against 16 and the beat drifts.
  5. Hold REC while it plays: FILL.

Conditions

Hold a step and turn PRESETS, or press black 6–11 (100%, 50%, 1:2, 1:4, FILL, !FIL). The step's cell is framed; the held step's card names it.

Ratchets

Hold a step and press OCT+ or OCT−: one to four hits, evenly through the step, each with the step's trig type. The cell marks the extra hits along its foot.

Fills

Hold REC while it plays: FILL steps play, !FIL steps rest. GLO + white 7: FILL for the next loop only.

The step tools

Hold SEQ: the white keys are tools for the track in view, over its length, its locks moving with its steps. GLO + OCT− undoes one.

  • White 2 plays the steps backwards, 3 and 4 shift them a step, 5 throws in a random quarter of the steps, 7 moves a few notes a tone, a third, a fourth or a fifth.
  • To give another track the same steps: hold SEQ, press REC; turn SELECT; hold SEQ, press PLAY.

Lengths, speeds, chances

Press HOME until the footer says HOME 2: the track in view's TLEN, SPD, PROB and LANE (SEQ held turns TLEN and SPD too).

  • TLEN: the track loops over its own length, PAT for the pattern's. 16 against 12 against 7 drift apart, and all start together again at PLAY and at the next pattern.
  • SPD: 1/8×, 1/4×, 1/2×, 3/4×, 1×, 3/2×, 2×. 3/4× against 1× for a lilt, 1/2× for a long line.
  • PROB: each trig of the track plays with that chance, after its own condition: 100 % down to 0 % in 16 steps. A 1:2 trig at 50 % plays one loop in four. On the lanes a trig under 100 % fills to its chance.

Grids

Mutable Instruments' topographic drum sequencer, on any track. Press ARP again for GRIDS, then LANES: a map of 25 drum patterns, X and Y a place on it.

PageKnobs
GRIDSX, Y; CHAOS varies each cycle; RES 16: a bar, 32: two bars.
LANESBD, SD, HH: each lane's density. LANE: the lane this track follows.
  • A track on a lane plays its trigs, not its steps: its own sound, at its root, Grids' accents, on Grids' cycle, at the track's speed. CHAOS is the same at every PLAY.
  • Stopped, OCT+ on GRIDS or LANES writes the lanes into those tracks' steps and lets go of the lanes.

Patterns into a song

Sixteen patterns a bank, four banks: each pattern a part with its own sounds. Chain them as you play.

  1. Hold HOME and press REC: the open pattern is copied. Tap an empty pattern's key and press PLAY: pasted there, and open.
  2. Still holding HOME, press SEQ: its steps clear, its six sounds stay. Write the next part.
  3. Playing, turn PRESETS on HOME, or hold HOME and tap a key: the next part starts at the end of the bar. The same key again before it: now.
  4. Mute and unmute tracks with GLO + white 1–6: each change lands on the next bar.

Choosing patterns

  • PRESETS on HOME and GLO: at once when stopped, at the end of the bar while playing.
  • HOME held: white 1–16 the bank's patterns, black 1–4 the bank (A to D). Each opens (playing: queued for the bar's end) and is picked.

Mutes on the bar

GLO > PLAY's MUTE: BAR (the default) holds a mute made while playing to the next bar's first step; NOW mutes at once. Stopped, always at once. The key blinks while it waits.

Play it live

Mutes, solos, fills, the bus's effects and sweeps that come back, under one hand.

  1. Double-tap GLO: white 1–6 mute, 9–14 solo while held, 7 fills the next loop, 8 taps the tempo, 15 unmutes all. The keys light for the tracks heard.
  2. Hold FX for PERFORM: a black key repeats, reverses, stops or kills the mix while held. OCT+ for bank B. Double-tap FX to keep it under both hands.
  3. Hold LFO and sweep a knob: it comes back when you let go.
  4. Tap the latched button again to let go.

PERFORM

The black keys play the bus's effects while held; the white keys still play the track. OCT− is bank A, OCT+ bank B; a held effect keeps going when you switch. The knobs are the bus's: DCAY, DAMP, ADPC and DRIV. Nothing clicks, and the repeats land on the beat even if you press late.

Double-tap: a layer latched

Tap GLO, SEQ, HOME or FX twice, quickly: the layer stays as if you held it. Its button blinks and its map says LATCHED. The keys and the knobs are the layer's; copy, paste and clear still want the button held. Tap it again, or any other page button, to let go.

  • GLO latched: mutes, solos, the fill and the tap tempo under one hand.
  • HOME latched: patterns picked and queued as you play.
  • FX latched: PERFORM with both hands on the keys.
  • SEQ latched: the tools and the sequencer's knobs.

Solos and the tempo

GLO + white 9–14 solo tracks 1–6 while held, several adding up; playing, from the next bar, and a tap solos one bar. GLO + white 8 taps the tempo. Hold LFO for sweeps that come back; OCT+ while held keeps the change.

Save, undo, share

SAVE keeps the pattern; held, the project. GLO + OCT− undoes.

  1. SAVE: the pattern as it is. What you play is a working copy until then.
  2. GLO + SAVE: back to the last saved version.
  3. Hold SAVE: the whole project.
  4. GLO + OCT− undoes the last copy, paste, clear, INIT, reload or step tool; GLO + OCT+ does it again.

Projects

Four projects of four banks (A–D) of sixteen patterns, and a library of 128 sounds. On the FM-1: one project, A the demos and B–D INIT. In the browser GLO > SET picks a project (PROJ) and loads it (LOAD, then OCT+ for YES); the first, DEMO, holds the factory's bank A.

Files

  • The emulator: under the panel, a project exported as a .m2kp file and imported into any of the four slots.
  • The FM-1: the Patch Editor reads its patterns and sounds over USB and keeps them as a .m2ks file.

The Patch Editor

Read the FM-1's patterns and sounds over USB, edit them in the browser, send them back, keep them as a file.

The Patch Editor talks to an FM-1 running MONO2K over its USB cable, in Chrome or Edge. It edits the FM-1's patterns as their own session, apart from this browser's saves, which stay as they are.

  1. Connect the FM-1 by USB and open the Patch Editor. Press Start, then Connect, and allow MIDI with SysEx when the browser asks. Close the Installer if it is open in another tab.
  2. Stop the FM-1 (PLAY). Press Read from FM-1: it saves what it holds and sends its 64 patterns and 128 sounds (a few seconds). The pattern list shows them as the FM-1 has them.
  3. Edit: every parameter of every track, heard as you move it, the FM-1's own patterns playing in the browser.
  4. Press Send to FM-1 and confirm: the FM-1 takes them, plays them, and keeps them in its flash.

Files

Save file keeps what is being edited as a .m2ks file: the FM-1's patterns and sounds (mono2k-fm1- and the day) or this browser's projects. Open file reads one back: an FM-1 file as the FM-1's session, ready to send. Back to this browser's saves returns to the emulator's work.

Safety

The parts of a transfer arrive in order, each answered, and the whole is checked against its CRC before the FM-1 uses it. A transfer that fails, stalls or is cut off leaves the FM-1 with what it had. While the FM-1 plays it neither sends nor takes. A transfer touches only the patterns and sounds, never the firmware.

Clock and sync

GLO > MIDI. SYIN (sync in) follows a MIDI clock: its tempo, start, continue, stop and the song position, from the input you pick: OFF, USB, TRS or BOTH; the other input's clock and transport are ignored. SYOU (sync out) sends MONO2K's clock, 24 pulses a beat, with start and stop; following a clock, it passes that one on. NOTE (notes out) sends each track's trigs on its channel. With SYIN off, start and continue still play and stop still stops, at MONO2K's own tempo, from where the notes come (NOTE on GLO > IN).

The clock comes in over USB, from a computer, phone or iPad, or on the TRS jack from any MIDI device. The FM-1's USB port is a device, like a keyboard's, and its TRS jack is an input only, so two FM-1s cabled to each other don't hear each other. Put a host between them: a computer or an iPad with a DAW or a MIDI router, or a USB MIDI host box. The one that leads must send a clock: MONO2K does (SYOU on); set the other's SYIN to USB.

USB and TRS

GLO > IN keeps the FM-1's two inputs apart. NOTE (notes in) picks where notes, controllers and mutes are taken from: BOTH, USB, TRS or OFF. THRU sends what comes in on the TRS jack out over USB as it came (notes, controllers, programs, the clock, start, stop and the song position), so the FM-1 is also a MIDI interface for the device on its jack; what comes in over USB never goes back. With SYOU on, the jack's clock, start and stop are not passed (MONO2K sends its own, or passes on the one it follows), so the computer hears one clock.

A DAW over USB and a keyboard on the jack, for instance: SYIN USB follows the DAW, NOTE TRS plays MONO2K from the keyboard alone, THRU on records the keyboard in the DAW too. On the page each input's square lights as MIDI comes in, and what it feeds is filled. In the emulator, the computer's MIDI comes in as USB.

Commands

Every button and combination on one page: the cheat sheet. The jobs step by step are under Make a track.

Six rules

  1. REC copies, PLAY pastes, SEQ clears. The button you hold says what: GLO the pattern (in GRID, the track's steps), HOME the patterns you pick, EDIT the track's sound, a step held in GRID that step.
  2. Again undoes. The same paste or clear a second time puts things back (on one pattern; several at once stay done). GLO + OCT− undoes the last edit, GLO + OCT+ redoes it.
  3. Hold the clear for INIT. Keep SEQ down a second: a ring fills on the screen and the pattern (or the patterns, or the track) goes INIT.
  4. Only the flash asks. Writing a sound over another, wiping every pattern, the demos back and loading a project ask first: OCT+ YES, OCT− NO.
  5. Tap for the page, hold for the layer. Double-tap GLO, SEQ, HOME or FX and the layer stays without holding.
  6. Held is for now. Solos, PERFORM's effects and LFO's knobs last while held; mutes, tools and edits stay.

The FM-1's CPU

What the FM-1 can carry, how it shows its load, and what it does when it is full.

The load

GLO > PLAY shows it, every half second: CPU, the first core's share of real time making the sound; CPU2, the second core's; DROP, the times the sound ran short since power-on. In the browser, dashes. Each track's cell has a bar of what that track takes (full at 50 %). The FM-1 shares the tracks between its two processors by what each takes there, measured as it plays, and the bus runs on the second a block behind, so the sound comes out 0.36 ms later.

When it is full

Rather than crack, the FM-1 gives way a step at a time, the least heard first. Each step waits for the last to take effect.

  1. The screen. Its frames come further apart. The sound is untouched.
  2. A tail. A note that has ended and only rings (its release, its echoes) fades out in 3 ms. Its next note plays as ever. Its cell says CUT for a moment.
  3. A track rests. It fades out in 3 ms, its notes silent; its cell says REST. It plays again from its next note once there has been room for a second.

A drop the sound did not cause (a flash write, a page turned) only moves the screen: nothing is cut for it.

REST: which track

GLO > PLAY, KNOB 2. FM-1 only: the browser has no limit, so there it does nothing.

AUTOThe quietest for what it costs; a hit every beat counts as loud. Never the track you are on. The default.
T1–T6That track rests first, even the one you are on. Then AUTO.
NONENo track rests. The screen and tails still give way; past them the sound may drop, and DROP counts it.

CPU FULL says what it did, at most every 5 seconds: T2 TAIL CUT, or T2 RESTS, T4 HEAVIEST. Lighten the heaviest one.
