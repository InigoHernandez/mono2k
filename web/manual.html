Introduction

MONO2K turns the M-VAVE FM-1 into a small Monomachine: six mono machines, a 64-step sequencer that locks any parameter to any step, and a lofi bus that breaks the sound on purpose.

Each of the six tracks plays one machine: a sine, two kinds of noise, a chip, three kinds of FM, a supersaw and a pulse, a chord, digital waves, a voice, and three FX machines that work on the track before them. Every track has the Monomachine's chain after its machine: an amp envelope, a band of filters, distortion, an EQ, rate reduction and a delay of its own, then MONO2K's bits, wavefold and XOR.

The sequencer is the Monomachine's, with the modern Elektron conditions: each step trigs the amp, the filter's envelope and the LFOs independently, carries locks, a chance or an A:B condition, and up to four ratchets.

MONO2K 1.0

The same firmware runs in the browser and on the FM-1; install it from The Firmware Hub or the installer: see Status and safety.

Finding a function

On the FM-1, hold GLO, HOME, EDIT, SEQ, FX or LFO for a moment: the screen lists what each does held with the other buttons and the keys; hold any other button a second for its card. Every combination is on one page here: Commands; the common jobs step by step: Workflows.

Status and safety

What runs where today, and what installing will mean.

Today

MONO2K is at 1.0. Its C core runs in your browser on the emulator page and, in the FM-1 build, on the FM-1 itself, on the open FM-1 platform (Felucca's, through CS-1): the same sound, sequencer and screen in both. MONO2K 1.0 for the FM-1 installs from The Firmware Hub or the installer page. On the FM-1 it keeps one project of 64 patterns (bank A the demos, B–D INIT, with room for some 20 to 30 patterns of your own beside the demos; it says when it is full) and the 128 sounds; the emulator keeps four projects of 64.

Saves are kept in your browser and can be exported as files; the FM-1 keeps its own in its flash, and the Patch Editor reads them and sends them back (The FM-1 and the Patch Editor). When the save format grows, older saves are converted as they load.

Installing

The FM-1 build installs from the Installer page, over USB, in Chrome or Edge, as Felucca does. Back up what your FM-1 holds first; installing other firmware is at your own risk. M-VAVE's own updater, or the installer's return to the official firmware, takes the FM-1 back to how it shipped; holding OCT− while it powers on always reaches the installer.

Note

MONO2K is not affiliated with Elektron or M-VAVE. Monomachine is Elektron's; FM-1 is M-VAVE's. MONO2K's machines are written from scratch and measured against a Monomachine's output; none of Elektron's code or samples are in it.

MIDI and saving

A MIDI keyboard or controller plugged into the computer plays the emulator as it would the FM-1 (see MIDI). Under the panel, the project's tools export a project as a .m2kp file and import one into any of the four project slots.

Commands

Every button and combination on one page. On the FM-1, hold GLO, HOME, EDIT, SEQ, FX or LFO for a moment and the screen shows its own; hold any other button a second for its card.

Six rules

  1. REC copies, PLAY pastes, SEQ clears. The button you hold says what: GLO the pattern (in GRID, the track's steps), HOME the patterns you pick, EDIT the track's sound, a step held in GRID that step.
  2. Again undoes. The same paste or clear a second time puts things back (on one pattern; several at once stay done). GLO + OCT− undoes the last edit, GLO + OCT+ redoes it.
  3. Hold the clear for INIT. Keep SEQ down a second: a ring fills on the screen and the pattern (or the patterns, or the track) goes INIT.
  4. Only the flash asks. Writing a sound over another, wiping every pattern, the demos back and loading a project ask first: OCT+ YES, OCT− NO.
  5. Tap for the page, hold for the layer. Double-tap GLO, SEQ, HOME or FX and the layer stays without holding (below).
  6. Held is for now. Solos, PERFORM's effects and LFO's knobs last while held; mutes, tools and edits stay.

Double-tap: a layer latched

Tap GLO, SEQ, HOME or FX twice, quickly: the second tap undoes the first (the page, GRID) and the layer stays as if you held it. Its button blinks and its map says LATCHED. The keys and the knobs are the layer's; the button combinations (copy, paste, clear) still want the button held, so PLAY, REC, SAVE and the octaves do their own job meanwhile. Tap the button again to let go; any other page button lets go too. Tapping a page button slowly steps through its pages as before.

  • GLO latched: mutes, solos, the fill and the tap tempo under one hand.
  • HOME latched: patterns picked and queued as you play.
  • FX latched: PERFORM with both hands on the keys.
  • SEQ latched: the tools and the sequencer's knobs.

Cards and lights

Hold SEL, ENV, ARP, PLAY, OCT− or OCT+ a second, touching nothing else, and the screen shows what it does alone and with the others; letting go then does nothing more.

The keys light for the layer held: with GLO, white 1–6 for the tracks heard and 9–14 for those soloed; with HOME, the bank's patterns (lit used, the open one winking, the queued one blinking) and the bank on the black keys; with FX, the effects on; with SEQ, the eight tools. In GRID, steps with a trig are lit, the playhead too.

Workflows

The common jobs, step by step.

A new pattern from nothing

  1. Hold HOME, press black 2 (G#3) for bank B, and tap a white key: an INIT pattern opens.
  2. Press EDIT and turn ALGORITHM to choose each track's machine, or load sounds from the library (GLO + EDIT).
  3. Press SEQ, place trigs with the white keys, PLAY. SAVE keeps it.

A new pattern on the same sounds

  1. Hold HOME: REC copies the open pattern; tap the key of an empty one and press PLAY to paste it there.
  2. Still holding HOME, press SEQ: its steps clear, its six sounds stay.

Empty one pattern, several, or all

  1. One: hold GLO and press SEQ: the steps go, the sounds stay. Keep SEQ down a second for INIT. SEQ again puts it back.
  2. One track: hold REC and tap SEQ: the track in view's steps go, the others stay.
  3. Several: hold HOME, tap their keys (black 1–4 for the other banks), press SEQ; keep it down for INIT.
  4. All: GLO > SET, turn WIPE, OCT+ for YES. On the FM-1, DEMO brings bank A's demos back.

Undo a slip

  1. GLO + OCT− undoes the last copy-paste, clear, INIT, reload or SEQ tool; GLO + OCT+ does it again.
  2. A paste or a clear repeated undoes itself too.

Keep a sound, find it again

  1. On the track: hold EDIT and press SAVE. It goes to the first INIT slot, in the category it sounds like.
  2. To file it under a category yourself: GLO + EDIT, turn CAT to one, pick an INIT slot with SLOT, turn SAVE.
  3. To load: GLO + EDIT, CAT to the kind you want, SLOT through them, turn LOAD.
  4. To start a track afresh: hold EDIT, press SEQ.

Put a knob back

  1. Hold OCT− and turn the knob: it goes back to its machine's default.
  2. In GRID with a step held, the same takes that knob's lock away from the step.

Play live with one hand

  1. Double-tap GLO. White 1–6 mute, 9–14 solo while held, 7 fills the next loop, 8 taps the tempo; the keys light for the tracks heard.
  2. Hold FX for PERFORM: a black key repeats, reverses, stops or kills the bus while held. Double-tap FX to keep it under both hands.
  3. Hold LFO and sweep a knob: it comes back when you let go.
  4. Tap the latched button again to let go.

Play the tracks as drum pads

  1. GLO + ARP: PADS in the header. White keys 1–6 (and 9–14 for the other hand) hit tracks 1–6, each at its root: the note its trigs sit on most in the pattern, so a drum stays in the key it is tuned to (a track with no trigs plays the note you last played on it).
  2. Press PLAY, tap REC and play: each hit is written to the nearest step of its track.
  3. GLO + ARP again, or SEQ, ends it.

Add a bar

  1. Press SEQ for GRID. Hold the next page key (P2 is A#4) a moment: the pattern grows by a bar, a copy of the ones before. Or hold SEQ and press white 6: twice as long.
  2. OCT+ shows the new bar; change it. The footer shows the bars, the one in view solid.

Move a track's beat around

  1. Hold SEQ: white 3 and 4 shift the track's steps a step earlier or later, white 2 plays them backwards, white 5 throws in a random quarter of the steps, white 7 moves a few notes.
  2. To give another track the same steps: hold SEQ, press REC (copy); turn SELECT to the other track; hold SEQ, press PLAY (paste).
  3. To run one track slower or faster than the rest: hold SEQ and turn KNOB 2 (3/4× against 1× for a lilt, 1/2× for a long line).

Vary the steps quickly

  1. In GRID, hold a step (or several) and press a black key: 1–5 its trig type, 6–11 its condition (50%, 1:2, 1:4, FILL, !FIL).
  2. Hold REC while it plays for FILL, or GLO + white 7 for a fill on the next loop.

Send your patterns to MONO2K

  1. Stop the FM-1. In the Patch Editor: Start, Connect, Read from FM-1, Save file.
  2. Send the .m2ks file to the author (The FM-1 and the Patch Editor).

Pages

Eight pages, four knobs at a time. A button opens its page; pressed again it turns to the page's next four knobs.

The pages and their knobs

Read off the firmware: the SYN page is each machine's own (see Machine reference).

Tracks and machines

Six tracks, each one mono machine with its own sound, chain and steps.

Choosing a track and its machine

SELECT picks the track; in GRID, the upper keys T1–T6 do. ALGORITHM changes the track's machine: its card comes up, and its eight knobs start from the machine's own settings while the rest of the track (the chain, the LFOs, the steps) stays as it was. The machines come in the Monomachine's order, its three FM+ machines together.

Level, pitch and mute

On HOME, KNOB 1 and 2 are the track's level and pitch. In GRID the MUTE key mutes the track in view; GLO held with one of the six lower keys mutes that track from anywhere.

FX machines

FX-CHORUS, FX-RING and FX-DYNAMIX make no sound of their own: each takes the track before it (after that track's amp) and works on it. Put the source on track 3 and the FX on track 4; the FX hears the source before its VOL, so turn the source's VOL down to hear only the effect. The Monomachine's metal sounds are made this way: a plucky FM+PAR into FX-CHORUS with its feedback all the way up.

A sound, copied

On a track's page, GLO + REC copies the track's sound (its machine and every setting) and GLO + PLAY pastes it onto the track in view, in this pattern or another. On HOME the same two copy and paste the whole pattern.

Patterns and steps

Sixteen patterns a bank, each a whole song part with its own sounds; six tracks of up to 64 steps.

SEQ turns the keys into steps. The sixteen lower keys are the steps of the page in view; OCT− and OCT+ turn the page (four pages, 64 steps). A key on an empty step puts a trig there with the last note you played; a quick tap on a trig takes it away, with its locks.

  1. Press SEQ: GRID shows in the header.
  2. Pick the track with SELECT, or with the upper keys T1–T6.
  3. Tap the lower keys to place trigs. Hold one to edit it.
  4. Press PLAY.

Choosing patterns

PRESETS picks the pattern: at once when stopped, at the end of the bar while playing, so patterns chain as you play.

An accent plays the step harder (GLO > TIME's ACNT says how much); a slide glides into the next note without trigging again.

Parameter locks

Any knob, locked to any step: the Monomachine's way of making one track sound like many.

Hold a step in GRID and turn any of the four knobs, on any page: the value is locked to that step, and plays for that step alone. The knob shows the lock in reverse, and the step's cell turns checkered (with a condition as well, a mark in its corner). A step can carry locks on as many parameters as you like, up to the pattern's pool of locks.

Locks can change a machine's knobs, the chain, the LFOs, anything with a knob on a track's page. Taking the trig away takes its locks with it.

While recording live, a knob turned just after a trig plays locks its value to that step; between trigs it changes the sound itself.

To lock without trigging anything, give the step the trig type NONE: it moves the pitch and applies its locks, and restarts nothing.

Trig types

The Monomachine's magic: each step trigs the amp, the filter's envelope and the LFOs independently.

A synthesizer usually restarts all its envelopes on every note. On MONO2K, as on the Monomachine, a step can restart some of them and leave the others running: change the pitch under a filter sweep that keeps going, restart only the LFO for a new wobble, or move the note with no new attack at all.

The eight states

Hold a step in GRID and turn MASTER (in the browser, press /) to step through them.

Every type moves the pitch to the step's note and applies the step's locks. The machine's own envelopes (an FM+ operator falling from the trig, GND-SIN's pitch drop) restart with the amp, as on the Monomachine. Without AMP the note's gate runs on as it was: if the amp has already died away, a FILTER or LFO trig changes a sound you cannot hear. NONE is a trigless step: a pitch change and a set of locks, with nothing restarted.

Only the LFOs set to restart on a trig restart (LFO page, TRIG). Ratchets repeat the step with the same type. Notes played on the keys or over MIDI always trig ALL.

Ideas

  • A bass line with one long filter sweep: ALL on the first step, AMP on the others.
  • A held pad whose chord changes under one envelope: NONE on the chord changes.
  • A wobble that starts again on the off-beats: LFO on the steps between the notes.

Conditions and ratchets

After the modern Elektron sequencers: steps that play by chance, by loop, or on FILL; steps that repeat.

Hold a step and turn PRESETS. The strip draws a step on a condition framed, its block inset; the held step's card names the condition.

Ratchets

Hold a step and press OCT+ or OCT−: the step plays one to four hits, evenly through its length, each with the step's trig type. The strip marks the extra hits along the cell's foot.

Recording

Live while the pattern plays, or step by step while it is stopped.

Live

With the keys playing notes (not GRID), press PLAY and tap REC. Notes you play land on the nearest step; a hard key records an accent. Knobs turned while recording lock their value to the step just played. Tap REC again to stop recording. A take you don't want: hold REC and tap SEQ, and the track's steps are gone (SEQ again brings them back).

Step entry

With the keys playing notes and the sequencer stopped, tap REC: STEP ENTRY. Each note you play is written to the next step and the cursor moves on. EDIT leaves a rest; PRESETS moves the cursor back and on. Tap REC to leave.

FILL

Hold REC while playing for FILL: steps with the FILL condition play, steps with !FIL rest.

The track chain

Every track has the Monomachine's chain after its machine, then MONO2K's destroy effects.

Amp

ATCK, HOLD, DEC and REL shape each note, on the Monomachine's measured curves: HOLD keeps the note at full level before it decays, so most sounds need no note-off; it counts in beats at the pattern's tempo (32 is a beat, 8 a sixteenth), as on the Monomachine. DIST drives the machine into a clip of its own, VOL sets the track's level into the chain, PAN places it, PORT glides between notes.

Filter

A high-pass and a low-pass as one band: BASE is the bottom of the band, WDTH its width (at the top, BASE alone is a high-pass; at the bottom, WDTH alone is a low-pass), HPQ and LPQ their resonance. The filter follows the note. Its envelope (ATCK, DEC) moves the base and the width by B-OF and W-OF.

FX

EQF and EQG are one band of EQ; SRR reduces the sample rate; the delay (DTIM, DSND, DFB, DBAS, DWID) is the track's own, in 8-bit mu-law. DSTR: BITS crushes, FOLD folds the wave over itself, XOR mangles the samples, VERB sends the track to the bus's reverb. MOD is a chorus, a flanger or a phaser across the stereo.

LFOs

Three per track, two pages each: TRCK and DEST say which track and parameter it moves (any track's), WAVE its shape, TRIG what a trig does to it (FREE runs on, TRIG starts it over, HOLD samples it until the next trig, ONE runs one cycle, HALF half a cycle), MULT and SPD its rate in time with the tempo, PHAS where it starts, DPTH how far, either way. An LFO on pitch moves it in coarse steps; for vibrato aim it at the machine's TUNE.

Arpeggiator

On ARP: MODE (OFF, UP, DOWN, U/D, RND, ORDR, the order you played them), SPD (1/32 to 1/4), RNG (one to four octaves) and GATE. Hold keys and it plays them in turn.

The bus

Where the six tracks meet: a reverb, a beat repeat, tape, ADPCM, hiss and a drive into the soft clip.

Saving and projects

Four projects of four banks (A–D) of sixteen patterns, and a library of 128 sounds. On the FM-1: one project of four banks, A the demos and B–D INIT.

Patterns

SAVE keeps the pattern as it is; GLO + SAVE goes back to the last saved version. What you play is your working copy: you can change it freely and keep it or not.

Clearing takes the steps away and leaves every sound: the machines, the knobs, the chain, the LFOs; holding the clear a second makes the pattern INIT, and clearing again puts it back. GLO + OCT− undoes any of them. Holding HOME does the same to several patterns at once (Workflows).

The sound library

128 sounds, S001 to S128, kept apart from the patterns: a sound saved there can go onto any track of any pattern. A sound is a track's machine and every setting (the machine's knobs, the amp, the filter, the FX, the LFOs) and a category: KICK, SNARE, HATS, PERC, BASS, LEAD, PAD or FX. The library starts with the demos' 24 sounds, each in the category its part gives it, then 104 INIT slots; it is saved with the rest.

Press EDIT until the header says SND (the page after SYN, and after BANK on DIGI-WAV), or GLO + EDIT from anywhere.

The factory's bank A

Four parts, each a 4-bar pattern that plays as a 16-bar form: what comes in after the first pass, what alternates, what arrives on the third and fourth. Every sound in them was picked by ear from sound sheets of random settings, then written into parts and fixed by measurement until it sat in the tempo and the harmony. A05–A16 are blank, ready for yours.

The FM-1 and the Patch Editor

Read the FM-1's patterns and sounds over USB, edit them in the browser, send them back, keep them as a file.

The Patch Editor talks to an FM-1 running MONO2K over its USB cable, in Chrome or Edge. It edits the FM-1's patterns as their own session, apart from this browser's saves, which stay as they are.

  1. Connect the FM-1 by USB and open the Patch Editor. Press Start, then Connect, and allow MIDI with SysEx when the browser asks. Close the Installer if it is open in another tab.
  2. Stop the FM-1 (PLAY). Press Read from FM-1: it saves what it holds and sends its 64 patterns and 128 sounds (a few seconds). The pattern list shows them as the FM-1 has them.
  3. Edit: every parameter of every track, heard as you move it, the FM-1's own patterns playing in the browser.
  4. Press Send to FM-1 and confirm: the FM-1 takes them, plays them, and keeps them in its flash.

Files

Save file keeps what is being edited as a .m2ks file: the FM-1's patterns and sounds (mono2k-fm1- and the day) or this browser's projects. Open file reads one back: an FM-1 file as the FM-1's session, ready to send. Back to this browser's saves returns to the emulator's work.

Safety

The parts of a transfer arrive in order, each answered, and the whole is checked against its CRC before the FM-1 uses it. A transfer that fails, stalls or is cut off leaves the FM-1 with what it had. While the FM-1 plays it neither sends nor takes. A transfer touches only the patterns and sounds, never the firmware.

Sending patterns to MONO2K

Made a part you like on the FM-1? Read it into the Patch Editor, Save file, and send the .m2ks to the author: patterns made on the FM-1 may become the next factory demos.

Clock and sync

GLO > MIDI. SYIN (sync in) follows a MIDI clock: its tempo, start, continue, stop and the song position. SYOU (sync out) sends MONO2K's clock, 24 pulses a beat, with start and stop. NOTE (notes out) sends each track's trigs on its channel. With SYIN off, start and continue still play and stop still stops, at MONO2K's own tempo.

The clock comes in over USB, from a computer, phone or iPad, or on the TRS jack from any MIDI device. The FM-1's USB port is a device, like a keyboard's, and its TRS jack is an input only, so two FM-1s cabled to each other don't hear each other. Put a host between them: a computer or an iPad with a DAW or a MIDI router, or a USB MIDI host box. The one that leads must send a clock: MONO2K does (SYOU on); set the other to follow its USB clock.
