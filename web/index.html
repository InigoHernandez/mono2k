Six mono machines

A Monomachine-style synthesizer for a palm-sized FM synth. Six tracks, each one machine: sine, noise, chip, three kinds of FM, supersaw and pulse, chord, digital waves, a voice, and three FX machines. A 64-step sequencer with parameter locks, the Monomachine's trig types, conditions and ratchets. A bus that breaks the sound beautifully.

    01

    Controls

    Solid labels are computer keys; the F-keys work too, F1 FX to F10 SEQ. Click the panel first, Esc leaves it. Drag or scroll a knob to turn it.

    Keys

    Z–M Q–O
    The 16 lower keys: notes, or the steps in GRID (a new step takes the last note played).
    S D F H J 2…9
    The 11 upper keys: notes, or in GRID tracks 1–6, pages 1–4 (held: the pattern ends there), mute.
    Step + knob
    A parameter lock.
    Step + [ ]
    Its condition: chance, 1:2 … 4:4, 1ST, PRE, FILL.
    Step + - =
    Its ratchet, 1–4 hits.
    Step + ; '
    Its note, a semitone a click.
    Enter REC, stopped
    Step entry: each note played is the next step. 5 EDIT a rest, [ ] the cursor.
    Step + , .
    Accent, slide.
    Step + / or VOL
    Its trig type: ALL, AMP, FILTER, LFO, AMP+FLT, AMP+LFO, FLT+LFO, NONE.
    Step + upper keys
    1–5 its trig type, 6–11 its condition, at once.

    Buttons

    SYN: the machine's A–H.
    8 ENV
    AMP: envelope, distortion, pan.
    0 SEL
    FILT: base and width, resonance, envelope.
    P FX
    EQ and rate, delay, destroy.
    L LFO
    Three LFOs, two pages each.
    K ARP
    The track's arpeggiator.
    G GLO
    Tempo, lofi bus, SPU reverb, settings. Held: REC copy, PLAY paste, SEQ clear, - = undo and redo, mutes and solos on the keys.

    02

    Machines

    Nineteen machines in eight families, sixteen that sound and three FX that work on the track before them: after what is published about the Monomachine's, and four drums after the LXR's voices; written from scratch for MONO2K, integer only, to be tuned against the original by ear and by measurement. Point at a card and its mark turns, as on the screen.

    Every parameterEight pages

    03

    Sequencer

    Four projects of four banks (A–D) of sixteen patterns. As on the modern Elektron boxes, each pattern is a whole song part with its own sounds. SAVE keeps a pattern, GLO + SAVE reloads it, SAVE held keeps the project. GLO + REC, PLAY and SEQ copy, paste and clear.

    Six tracks of up to 64 steps. Hold a step and turn a knob to lock a value to it. Trig conditions: a chance, every A of B loops, the first loop, after the previous condition, or only on FILL. A chance is drawn as its own density of dither; ratchets repeat a step two to four times.

    As on the Monomachine, each step trigs the amp, the filter's envelope and the LFOs independently: hold a step and turn VOL for ALL, AMP, FILTER, LFO, any two, or NONE, a trigless step that moves the pitch and its locks and restarts nothing. The step shows a third filled for each trig it keeps.

    04

    Destroy

    Every track has the Monomachine's chain: HP and LP filters as a base and a width, distortion, an EQ band, sample rate reduction and a delay of its own in 8-bit mu-law. Then bits, a wavefold and an XOR that mangles the samples.

    The bus: an SPU-style reverb at 22 kHz, a beat repeat that slices the last sixteenth, tape wow and flutter, a 4-bit ADPCM crunch, hiss and dust, and a drive into the soft clip.

    05

    Status

    MONO2K 1.0. This page runs the firmware's C core as it is; the FM-1 build adds Felucca's platform (the hardware layer, USB, storage, the update loader and the installer). Install it on the FM-1 from The Firmware Hub or the installer. Older saves are converted as they load.

    Built on the open FM-1 work: Felucca by Leo Kuroshita (Hügelton Instruments), CS-1, X0X, FoMni and the others. Orbitron and Inter Tight under the SIL OFL. Not affiliated with Elektron or M-VAVE; Monomachine is Elektron's.
